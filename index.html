Standard conditions and how to read the table

    Effects show what the condition does to the fall of the round: a negative value means the round falls short by that many metres. To compensate, apply the opposite. Cross wind blowing left to right moves the round right by the given mils. Effects scale roughly linearly; negative deviations (colder, thinner, lower target) have the opposite sign.

        Laying the mortar

        The mortar is laid on the azimuth of fire with the referred deflection set on the sight. To engage a target on another azimuth, change the deflection: left add, right subtract. Deflection = referred deflection + (azimuth of fire − target azimuth).

        Elevation comes from the firing table for the chosen charge and the target range.

        Adjusting fire

        The observer's corrections are along their own line to the target (the OT direction), and move the previous aim point: right/left across that line, add/drop along it.

        Open the Target grid (T), enter the OT direction and click your previous aim point. Count the correction off the grid, then measure the new point with the Range arm and compute new data.

        Fire single rounds while adjusting. When the observer calls fire for effect, fire 3–4 rounds.

        Height difference (VI)

        The firing table assumes the target is at the mortar's altitude. Read the target's altitude off the contours and spot heights; the vertical interval VI = target altitude − mortar altitude.

        The column Target 10 m higher gives the effect on range (negative: the round falls short). Compensate by firing at a corrected range: corrected range = map range − (VI ÷ 10) × table value, then look up the elevation for the corrected range.

        Example: range 2400 m, VI +80 m, table value −4.0 → 2400 − 8 × (−4.0) = 2432 m. A target below the mortar (negative VI) gives a shorter corrected range.

        Near a charge's maximum range with a large VI the correction loses accuracy; choose a higher charge. Rounds that clip a hill between mortar and target burst on the hill.

        Polar and shift missions (magnetic)

        In these missions the observer reads directions off a compass: they are magnetic. Convert them with the declination diagram in the map corner before plotting or entering them in the target grid.

        Polar: "direction, distance, up/down" from the observer. Set the range arm pivot to T31, plot the grid direction and distance, then measure from the mortar. "Up 40" means the target is 40 m higher than the observer.

        Shift: "shift AB0001, direction, left/right, add/drop, up/down". Centre the target grid on the known point, oriented to the grid direction, and count off the shift.

        Later corrections use the same (magnetic) direction as their axis.

        Met corrections

        Standard conditions: no wind, air 15 °C, density 100 %, propellant 21 °C. With a met message, work out the effect of each deviation with the firing table (at the target range, for your charge):

        1. Wind components. With θ = wind direction − target azimuth: head wind = speed × cos θ (negative = tail wind), cross wind from the left = speed × sin(−θ).

        2. Range. Effect = head (or tail) wind × table value + (air temp − 15) × table value + (density − 100) × table value + (propellant temp − 21) × table value. Corrected range = map range − effect (− the VI effect). Look up the elevation for the corrected range.

        3. Deflection. Cross wind from the left × table value = drift right in mils. Correct the other way: drift right → add that many mils to the deflection (left add).

        The met message is rounded and a little stale, and the wind gusts; expect some residual error.
